How a Formula 1 car works

A Formula 1 car is the fastest road-racing machine on Earth: it can pass 350 km/h, and it can corner with more than five times its own weight pressing it into the track. You do not need to know anything about racing to follow this. Each of the five pages explains one system of the car, in plain words, with a model you can play with.

How the five parts fit together

  1. 1. The tub keeps the driver safe A carbon shell around the driver, with a crumple cone at the front.
  2. 2. The power unit makes the push A small turbo petrol engine helped by electric motors.
  3. 3. The air pushes the car down Wings and the underside make grip out of the air itself.
  4. 4. The gearbox and wheel are the controls Eight gears and a steering wheel that is a mini control room.
  5. 5. The wheels, springs and brakes touch the road Suspension, brakes and tyres turn all of that into speed and stopping.

How to use these pages

Each page has a drawing you can poke, sliders underneath, and a short guided tour that starts by itself.

Under the drawing, the list of parts tells you what each thing is. Hover a name to see where it is. Any word you do not know is in the short word list at the bottom.
