Chassis and Safety

In plain English

A Formula 1 car is built around a very strong, very light carbon-fibre tub that the driver sits inside. Everything else bolts onto it.

If the car hits a wall, the cone at the front crumples like a drinks can and soaks up the crash energy, while the tub stays whole. That is how drivers walk away from crashes that look terrifying.

Try this first

  1. Press Run the test. Watch the dark cone at the front squash while the cell around the driver stays intact.
  2. Switch the test to Halo. The Halo is the curved bar over the driver's head. Raise the load and see how little it bends.
  3. Turn the carbon skin down. You can then see the driver, seat, fuel tank and electronics inside the tub.

Words used here

Monocoque
French for 'single shell'. Here: one moulded carbon-fibre tub that is both the body and the safety cell.
Carbon fibre
Thin woven threads of carbon set in resin. It is far lighter than steel and, in the right layout, just as strong.
g (g-force)
A push measured in multiples of your own weight. 20 g means the driver feels twenty times heavier for an instant.
m/s
Metres per second. 15 m/s is 54 km/h, the speed of the official frontal crash test.
kJ (kilojoule)
A unit of energy. The test crash carries about the energy needed to lift a one-tonne car nine metres.
kN (kilonewton)
A unit of force. 100 kN is roughly the weight of ten tonnes.
